# Code in Nothing: Domain Language

**Organism:** An independently identified software entity that has an immutable birth genome, an executable phenotype, a lifecycle and ancestry. It is not a process, an LLM or a human-equivalent intelligence.

**Seed:** The validated initial genome and starting phenotype from which an organism is instantiated.

**Genome:** The five named hereditary policy groups: `base`, `driver`, `directive`, `maturity_point`, and `stop_station`. No environment or verifier field is a sixth gene.

**Phenotype:** The executable behavior expressed by a particular version of the organism's program and accepted capabilities. Genotype and phenotype must not be conflated.

**Growth:** A verified change to a living organism's retained capabilities. A longer program or higher proposal count does not itself constitute growth.

**Mutation:** A proposed, potentially inheritable change. Mutations are not accepted growth until verification gates pass.

**Offspring:** A new organism with new identity and a documented lineage whose starting hereditary material is derived from one or more parents, subject to inheritance gates.

**Lineage:** The directed ancestry relation between genome and capability artifacts, including evidence of the origin of inherited material.

**Capability:** A program artifact that satisfies a named formal specification under a stated verification scope. Several specifications of the same function are not automatically distinct functional capabilities.

**Abstraction:** A reusable, independently validated building block whose benefit is demonstrated across tasks rather than assumed from syntactic duplication.

**World:** An independently versioned family of formally defined challenges with admitted domain and task semantics. A world is not a gene of an organism.

**Challenge:** An immutable instance of a formal task specification against which a candidate may be checked.

**World evolution:** An independently admitted change to a world's challenge generation policy or specifications; increasing only input range does not establish semantic novelty.

**Co-evolution:** Feedback-driven change of both organism populations and world populations, with distinct objectives and independent acceptance criteria.

**World admission:** A decision that a proposed world meets validity, non-vacuity, feasible-witness and budget criteria. Lack of a witness within a budget yields unknown, not impossibility.

**Independent verifier:** The authority that checks admissibility, program correctness and promotion under externally fixed rules unavailable for modification by evaluated candidates.

**Evidence:** A content-linked record supporting a bounded claim, with traceable input, versions, outcome and limits. A digest alone is not an authenticated signature or proof of universal correctness.

**Heritability:** An approved transfer of a capability or growth policy into a child's birth state, backed by applicable replay evidence and lineage.

**Maturity:** Passing a declared, independently assessed set of objectives at a defined evaluation version. It is not global optimality.

**Plateau:** Lack of measured progress during a specified window and budget. It does not prove further progress impossible.

**Stop station:** The terminal lifecycle policy of a particular organism; a stop can happen without maturity.

**Sealed transfer set:** An evaluation collection not exposed to candidate generation or world selection during the experiment.

**Research claim:** An explicitly scoped finding supported by results and controls. A demonstration of mechanism is not a claim of scientific advantage.
